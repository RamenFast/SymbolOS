# A decision with two kinds of evidence

*Worked example, not a recorded deployment or a cost benchmark.*

Rhy asks: **“Which part is measured, and which part matters to you?”**

Suppose you are choosing between a local model and a hosted API. A benchmark can measure latency. It cannot decide how much you value convenience, control or keeping particular data local.

## Try it by hand

1. Write the decision in one sentence.
2. Record your own priorities and reservations in your own words. Do not infer somebody else's feelings or diagnose a body sensation.
3. Separately record measured facts: tested model, hardware, latency, quality criteria, data sent, and total cost assumptions.
4. Compare them. If the easy choice conflicts with a priority, name the tradeoff rather than manufacture agreement.
5. Record the decision, the unresolved questions and what would make you revisit it.

A useful note might say:

> I prefer local processing for these documents. I have not yet measured whether this machine meets the response-time requirement. I will benchmark a small representative task before choosing.

Local inference still costs hardware, electricity, maintenance and attention. A hosted service's price and data handling depend on the actual service and agreement. This example supplies no invented prices, throughput numbers, return-on-investment date or successful team vote.

## What this demonstrates

The SymbolOS heart/mind distinction is a writing aid: keep human priorities alongside technical evidence without substituting either for the other. No automatic feeling detector or completed correlation engine is demonstrated here.
